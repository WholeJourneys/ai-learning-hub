Five rules for safer everyday AI use

Safety is not a warning label on every AI topic. It is a practical habit: protect people and information, verify important work, and keep a person accountable.

Protect private informationDo not paste passwords, financial or medical details, or private customer data into an unfamiliar tool.
Verify before relyingCheck important facts against trustworthy sources, especially current travel, legal, health, or financial information.
Keep a human responsibleReview decisions and anything sent, published, booked, or changed on your behalf.
Look for bias and omissionsAsk whose perspective, place, or customer might be missing from the answer.
Get consent and be transparentAsk before recording or sharing someone’s information, and disclose meaningful AI assistance when it matters.

ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini are assistants powered by families of language models. Perplexity, Copilot, and Gemini Notebook package models with search, files, or workplace tools. You do not need to master all of them—pick the one closest to your real work.

How connections work

A connection lets an AI assistant use an outside service you authorize—such as Google Drive, Dropbox, Gmail, a calendar, or GitHub. Depending on the service, it may search, read, sync, or take an action.

Connect carefully

The exact menu changes by product and plan. In ChatGPT and Codex, connections may appear inside a plugin or app. Availability, account authorization, and permission to take actions are separate controls.

Start with a jobChoose one need, such as finding a document—not “connect everything.”
Use the official directoryOpen Plugins or Apps, choose the service, and select Connect when prompted.
Check the account and accessConfirm which account you are signing into and review every requested permission.
Begin read-onlyTest search and summary tasks before allowing anything to create, edit, send, or delete.
Name the source in your requestUse @ when available, or explicitly ask the assistant to use the connected service.
Review and removeCheck citations or source files, approve important actions, and disconnect tools you no longer use.

Good first connection exercises

Use low-risk material while you learn what the assistant can access and how reliably it follows boundaries.

Google Drive: “Use Google Drive to find the newest version of our visitor FAQ. Summarize it and cite the file. Do not edit anything.”
Gmail: “Find messages from this sender from the last 30 days and summarize the open questions. Do not draft or send a reply.”
Dropbox: “Find three files about this itinerary and tell me their names, dates, and likely purpose. Do not move or delete anything.”
Calendar: “Show my appointments next week and identify open two-hour blocks. Do not create or change events.”
Connection rule of thumbGrant the least access needed. Do not connect sensitive financial, medical, password, or private customer information just to experiment. A connection can only use the access granted to its signed-in account, but the outside service’s own terms and privacy rules also apply.
